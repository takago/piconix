## B5.1 List devices safely

```text
piconix# ls /dev
piconix# ls /dev/gpio
piconix# pins
piconix# cat /proc/fb
```

Start with read-only inspection; no external circuit is required. `/dev` exposes device operations, while `/proc` presents system state.

**Limits of observation:** `cat /proc/fb` reports framebuffer data, but does not prove the LED matrix is wired correctly. Hardware experiments are **optional**. On a properly connected circuit, follow the [device guide](../../reference/devices/DEVICES.md) for `fbctl`, GPIO, PWM, and I²C. Printing text to a terminal is not the same as controlling an actual pin.

## B5.2 Read/write and ioctl

Some device functions fit normal `open()`/`read()`/`write()` semantics. Configuration such as pin direction or mode may need `ioctl()`. The VFS forwards operations to device-specific code.

## B5.3 Verify wiring before hardware tests

Consult the [pin assignments](../../reference/hardware/PINS.md), [wiring](../../reference/hardware/WIRING.md), and [devices](../../reference/devices/DEVICES.md) references before controlling GPIO, PWM, I²C, or an LED matrix. Observe pin reservations and voltage limits. Reading existing virtual files is enough to complete the required lesson.

## B5.4 Practice and check

Compare a stored `/tmp` file with `/proc/fb`.

1. Where does their information come from?
2. Are `/dev` and `/proc` both ordinary persistent files?
3. Why do device operations require wiring and safety checks?

Learning record: Write down **what you ran / what you observed / why it happened**.
